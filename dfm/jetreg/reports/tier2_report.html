2. Key comparisons

F4 - tracks and cells combine. TC-graph (0.147) beats both T (0.158) and C-graph (0.157): charged and calorimeter constituents carry partially independent calibration information, and the shared backbone exploits both. This is the first quantitative payoff of the merge.
F5 - C1 answered on this dataset. JC-graph gains a lot of global accuracy over C-graph (MAE 0.158 vs 0.177) - the calibrated jet 4-vector does carry information the thinned cells lack - but the gap is NOT pileup conditioning: adding μ to C-graph changes nothing (F6). It is the thinned-away constituent information, exactly as the retention measurement predicted. And notably J's help does not extend to mid-bin resolution (JC JER slightly worse than C alone).
F7 - TJ matches TC on resolution with better NLL/MAE. Umar's input combo is genuinely strong; cells add nothing on top of T+J at this cell-thinning level (TJC check queued as a Tier-3 confirmation).
F8 - estimator gap confirmed: MAE training brings bulk closure to 3.6% RMS (best of all) with unchanged resolution. Recommendation: report medians from MAE-trained models (or apply numerical inversion to NLL models) in any final result.

3. Masking-pretraining validation

Cluster masking (ratio 0.15) on the S3 subset of ttbar_1000 (640 train / 160 val events, ~22k cells/event), reconstructing snr_scaled with the mask-indicator channel; graph and set encoder variants under identical protocols.

Honest caveats: reconstruction saturates for high-SNR cells and cannot recover the sign of negative-SNR cells; the cluster-vs-random RMS difference partly reflects population composition (random masking includes unpredictable lone noise cells), not only task difficulty. None of this blocks the Tier-4 transfer test - whether these representations help the downstream regression is exactly what the finetune/linear-probe comparison measures.
